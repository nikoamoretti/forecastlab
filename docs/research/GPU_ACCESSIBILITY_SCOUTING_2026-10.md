# Cheaper, more accessible consumer GPUs: scouting pass

**As of:** 2026-10-03. **Scope:** consumer graphics cards for gaming, creative work and local AI. Prices are US unless noted. **Prepared for:** ForecastLab.

**Labels.** **[M]** = a measured or officially reported figure. **[E: who]** = a third-party estimate. **[R]** = a report or rumour the company has not confirmed; only widely reported ones are included. **[AC]** = my calculation from cited inputs, with the method stated. **[AJ]** = my judgment. [S#] links resolve to section F.

## Summary

- **Prices are rising, not falling.** On 2026-09-28, the cheapest in-stock US price for current cards ran from MSRP to 3.5x MSRP. The RX 9070 GRE sold at its $549 MSRP, while the RTX 5090 was $6,995 against $1,999. The RTX 5070 was $819 (MSRP $549) and the Arc B580 $329 (MSRP $249) [M] [S1](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/lowest-gpu-prices-tracking). Newegg median prices for RTX 50 cards rose by as much as 39% between June and August 2026 [M] [S2](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/geforce-rtx-50-series-gpu-prices-spike-as-much-as-39-percent-as-blackwell-price-hikes-hit-the-us-rtx-5070-gets-a-36-percent-hike-rtx-5060-up-27-percent-at-the-median-of-newegg-listings).
- **Memory is the main driver.** TrendForce reported conventional DRAM contract prices up 45–50% quarter-on-quarter (QoQ) in 4Q25 and 93–98% in 1Q26. It forecasts +58–63% for 2Q26, +13–18% for 3Q26 and +10–15% for 4Q26 [S3](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260226-12937.html) [S4](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260601-13070.html) [S5](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260331-12995.html) [S6](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260703-13134.html) [S7](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260930-13258.html). Compounded, that puts 4Q26 at about 5.5–6.6x the 3Q25 level [AC]. GDDR6 8Gb spot averaged $12.29 in the week to 2026-09-28 [M] [S8](https://dramexchange.com/). That is about 5x the $2.44 of July 2025 [S9](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/new-rumor-suggests-8gb-radeons-could-get-usd20-price-hikes-16gb-usd40-rising-gddr6-spot-prices-add-fuel-to-the-gpu-pricing-fire) and close to the ~$13 crypto-era peak of February 2022 [S10](https://www.tomshardware.com/news/gddr6-vram-prices-plummet).
- **No new generation soon.** NVIDIA reportedly plans no new gaming GPU in 2026 and has delayed both an RTX 50 refresh and RTX 60 [R] [S11](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/02/06/news-nvidia-reportedly-plans-no-new-gaming-gpu-in-2026-amid-memory-tightness-first-time-in-30-years/). AMD's board partners expect RDNA 5 in H2 2027 or early 2028 [R] [S12](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/amds-rdna-5-gaming-gpus-are-coming-late-next-year-according-to-aibs-at-computex-manufacturers-expect-new-team-red-cards-in-the-second-half-of-2027-alongside-nvidia). Intel has reportedly cancelled its Xe3P gaming GPUs [R] [S13](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/intel-has-reportedly-killed-discrete-gaming-gpus-for-the-upcoming-xe3p-arc-celestial-family-gaming-gpu-remains-uncertain-even-for-the-next-gen-xe4-druid-lineup-that-lands-in-2027).
- **How long the squeeze lasts is contested.** Micron expects supply and demand to be "much tighter in calendar 2027 and 2028" than in 2026 and has "no line of sight" to balance [S14](https://www.cio.com/article/4229625/memory-squeeze-set-to-tighten-through-2028-micron-says.html). SK hynix calls 2027 the worst year, with demand above supply beyond 2030 [S15](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/dram/sk-hynix-says-2027-will-be-the-worst-year-for-memory-shortage-forecasts-crunch-to-last-until-2030-ceo-shares-grim-outlook-on-the-day-sk-hynix-gets-listed-on-nasdaq). Microsoft said console memory and storage prices had risen more than 2.5x and that it expects "another doubling by the fall of 2027" [S16](https://www.purexbox.com/news/2026/06/xbox-announces-worldwide-price-increases-for-series-xs-consoles). Against this, Acer's CEO expects PC prices to fall after mid-2027 as Chinese (CXMT) memory capacity arrives [S17](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Acer-CEO-says-memory-makers-are-hyping-the-2030-shortage-to-protect-margins-predicts-prices-fall-by-mid-2027.1406436.0.html).
- **[AJ] Price outlook.** A broad fall in US street prices before mid-2027 is unlikely. Three facts point that way:
  - contract prices are still forecast to rise in 4Q26 [S7](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260930-13258.html);
  - board partner PC Partner (Zotac, Inno3D, Manli) expects selling prices to rise further in H2 2026 [S18](https://www.pcpartner.com/attachment/ac/1786708541S1DJ7.pdf);
  - tariff risk points up (see B4).

  The earliest credible relief is H2 2027–2028, when new memory capacity and next-generation GPU launches could coincide. If memory normalizes, mid-range cards could fall back towards MSRP. That would be roughly 24–33% below today's prices, given street/MSRP ratios of 1.32–1.49 for the RTX 5070, RTX 5070 Ti and RX 9070 XT [AC from [S1](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/lowest-gpu-prices-tracking)]. Flagships have further to fall but compete with AI buyers for 32 GB cards. Scenarios are in section E.
- **The most usable accessibility levers today are not cheaper new cards.** Three things work now:
  - software that stretches existing hardware: FSR 4.1 backports [S19](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpu-drivers/amd-brings-official-fsr-4-1-support-to-rx-7000-series-gpus-int8-model-now-available-in-300-games-rdna-3-apus-also-getting-fsr-4-1-soon), neural texture compression [S20](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXNTC), 4-bit model formats [S21](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-oss/);
  - second-life hardware, such as ~$100–150 ex-mining PS5-APU boards [S22](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/usd100-steam-machine-uses-a-cut-down-ps5-apu-with-bazzite-diy-console-offers-60-fps-at-1080p-with-16gb-of-gddr6);
  - rented or streamed GPU time, such as an RTX 5090 at $0.69 per hour [S23](https://www.runpod.io/pricing).

**[AC] Illustration: an RTX 5070-class card at today's input prices.**
- Die: ~$80–114.
- 12 GB GDDR7: ~$120.
- Cooler and packaging: up to ~$100.

That totals ≈ $300–335 before PCB/VRM parts, assembly, die packaging and test, NVIDIA's margin on the kit, the board partner's margin (10–17%), the retailer's margin (~12–24%) and freight. On these figures, memory now costs about as much as or more than the GPU die on mid-range cards. That holds for the RTX 5070 ($120 memory vs $80–114 die) and the RX 9070 XT ($197 at GDDR6 spot vs $117–173 die). The largest unknown is the kit price NVIDIA and AMD charge partners.

### B4. US tariff status for graphics cards (HTS 8473.30.11)

- **Section 301 (China tariffs from 2018).** A specific exclusion covers "Printed circuit assemblies for rendering images onto computer screens ('graphics processing modules')", statistical number 8473.30.1180 [S69](https://www.ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Issue_Areas/Enforcement/Section%20301/Index%20of%20Exclusions%20for%20Extension%20FINAL.pdf). USTR extended all 178 remaining exclusions to **2026-11-10** [S70](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2025/november/ustr-extends-exclusions-china-section-301-tariffs-related-forced-technology-transfer-investigation). Without a further extension, Chinese-made cards would face a 25% tariff [S71](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/u-s-extends-china-gpu-tariff-exemption-until-august-31-motherboards-also-listed-in-section-301-exclusion).
- **IEEPA tariffs.** The Supreme Court struck these down on 2026-02-20 [S72](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/607/24-1287/).
- **Section 122 surcharge (2026-02-24 to 2026-07-24).** Its exemption list carried over the IEEPA exemptions for computers and semiconductors [S73](https://globaltradealert.org/blog/s122-exemption-structure-explained).
- **Section 301 "forced labor" tariffs (10–12.5%, from 2026-07-24).** Annex II excludes HTS 8471 and 8473.30.11/.20/.51/.91 [S74](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Press/Releases/2026/FLIP%20301%20Investigation%20Final%20Action%20FRN%207-23-26%20FINAL.pdf) [S75](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/july/ustr-takes-action-forced-labor-section-301-investigations).
- **Section 232 semiconductor tariffs.** Phase 1 (Proclamation 11002, 2026-01-14 [S76](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/policy/trump-administration-weighs-expanding-chip-tariffs-to-laptops-consoles-and-servers)) is a 25% duty on a narrow class of advanced chips that meet compute-performance and memory-bandwidth thresholds. It exempts uses that include non-data-center consumer applications [S77](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2026-0209-us-section-232-proclamation-imposes-25-percent-tariff-on-certain-semiconductors). The administration is weighing a broader Phase 2 that would cover products containing chips, such as laptops, consoles and servers [R: POLITICO via Tom's, 2026-08-27] [S76](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/policy/trump-administration-weighs-expanding-chip-tariffs-to-laptops-consoles-and-servers).
- **[AJ] Net:** consumer cards appear to carry no additional US duty today. The near-term tariff risk is upward: the Nov 10 expiry and Phase 2.
