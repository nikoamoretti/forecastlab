# Known limitations

- Binary questions only.
- One local user. No accounts, billing, or sharing.
- Mock search/model quality is deterministic theater for the pipeline, not a substitute for live research.
- Live providers inherit vendor outages, rate limits, and prompt brittleness.
- Strict schema-constrained OpenAI graph output reduces malformed response risk but cannot guarantee a valid or useful graph. A provider can still truncate, refuse, or return content that fails schema or graph-domain validation. These outcomes fail closed after one logical graph request; ForecastLab stores sanitized diagnostics, not the malformed response, and does not automatically repair or retry it.
- `three_track_strict_forecaster_v1` requests each track estimate with a strict OpenAI schema that fixes driver directions, bounds every numeric field, and limits driver citations to that track's supplied evidence identifiers. Its single repair request now names the failing field paths without echoing values. This addresses the Personal V1 pilot's 10-of-10 `invalid_structured_output:track_forecast` failures but has not yet been verified by a paid run; it does not repair truncation, refusals, or forecast quality. It is otherwise identical to `three_track_forecaster`, which stays the frozen comparator for the Personal V1 pilot and controlled V1 evaluation; that profile and other legacy-track profiles keep the original JSON-object request, and non-OpenAI vendors still receive JSON-object mode.
- Wayback coverage is incomplete; missing snapshots mean missing evidence, not proof of absence.
- Model pretraining can leak post-cutoff facts into backtests.
- Auto-rerun is disabled. Watches only mark a forecast stale. Reruns require user action.
- Manual evidence URL intake audits the fetched document and can make it available to the existing extractor on a later explicit run. Intake does not itself establish factual correctness, relevance, independence, or claim grounding, and it never creates an Evidence Claim. Backtest intake remains dependent on archive or supported immutable-source coverage.
- Reliability diagrams are withheld below 20 resolved rows. That threshold is not a calibration claim.
- Synthetic benchmarks cannot support product-level forecasting-quality claims.
- The first V1 comparison has only ten synthetic questions. Its Brier, log-loss, cost, latency, completion, and evidence-coverage outputs verify the experiment framework and cannot establish profile superiority.
- The latest bounded five-question live operational acceptance remains a hard 3-of-5 failure. It does not establish repeatable live reliability, forecasting quality, or calibration, and this offline release/preregistration work does not reinterpret that result.
- `private_v1_real_evaluation_release_v1` now requires two fresh, source-cited, role-separated Codex receipts per included question, deterministic outcome-blind reserve ordering, and a database-enforced one-shot test claim. This is procedural AI review only. It is not human review, independent validation, publication-grade certification, or evidence of accuracy/calibration. Model errors can agree across roles, licensing assertions remain non-legal metadata, and structural blinding is not encryption. No 60/40/100 release was reviewed or frozen and no real experiment was run. `private_v1_historical_evidence_release_v1` separately adds exact packet coverage, reviewed missingness, explicit pre-cutoff proof, content-addressed bundle verification, and offline worker isolation.
- `private_v1_real_evaluation_release_v2` is a private-internal policy only. It preserves V1's split, leakage, temporal, outcome-blinding, role-separation, deterministic receipt, and no-evidence controls, while treating licensing/source-use/redistribution fields as auditable non-legal metadata. V2 instead requires every review source to carry deterministic publisher, retrieval-time, date-or-explicit-unknown-state, content/extracted-text hash, and concise evidence-note provenance. It does not license redistribution, establish a certified corpus, or support public claims.
- V2 provenance restoration does not convert an H017 candidate into a reviewed or evaluation-ready question. It can only preserve an independently re-verified historical source receipt or an explicit terminal provenance failure. A sealed local runner provides OS-enforced typed role-input separation on macOS, not human independence or cryptographic secrecy; no real review artifact is produced until a separately authorized role workflow succeeds.
- Blinded execution and sealed scoring are separate typed payloads and services, not encryption. A local administrator with database access can read both; the guarantee is that outcome fields are structurally absent from the forecast-construction DTO and are joined only after terminal forecast persistence.
- Evidence coverage counts provenance-backed planned units, not whether each claim is substantively correct or decision-relevant. The private-V1 sufficiency gate is stricter than coverage, but it is still a deterministic provenance/diversity policy rather than a semantic truth or forecast-quality test.
- Startup applies Alembic to head and stops if a revision fails. Isolated unit tests may still use `create_all`. Back up `data/forecastlab.db` before upgrading an existing local file.
- Tracks run sequentially to stay SQLite-safe; they remain informationally independent.
- Benchmark retries reuse the same task, question, and run. They do not create replacement identities. Exhausted transient errors fail the job, run, task, and experiment.
- Experiment terminal states are `completed`, `completed_with_failures`, and `failed`. A running task after a permanently failed job is a defect.
- Budget reservations prevent ForecastLab from starting a call that cannot fit the **run-lifetime** ceiling. They are not a vendor-enforced hard dollar cap. A failed real-model request without usage keeps estimated input tokens, maximum output tokens, and both reserved costs. Search prices are estimated unless the provider reports a cost. Launch preflight now includes model and planned-search upper bounds, but both remain catalog estimates; unavailable required pricing fails closed rather than being treated as zero.
- Research Planner V3 preserves the bounded logical call path and forecast-call reserve while separating successful evidence-document targets from distinct candidate URL attempts. Smoke profile V3 can retain five results from the same search, schedule distinct hosts ahead of a second URL from an already-used host, and skip same-host URLs after a 401/403/451 without spending another fetch attempt. This can recover from one blocked host or pre-extraction failure without another search or extraction call, but it does not guarantee that a usable or topically strong source exists. The host block is deliberately node-local and run-local; there is no global reputation or suppression system. Unused model reservations and unused candidate allowance are audit values, not provider calls or ledger rows.
- Compact indexed graph transport reduces repeated field names and relationship identifiers for the bounded smoke path, then runs the unchanged canonical graph validation and approval rules. It does not improve graph reasoning or forecast quality. The immutable 1,536 visible-token and 4,200-character limits can still reject a complex but otherwise valid model response, and there is intentionally no automatic repair or second graph request.
- A queued benchmark experiment fails closed if application code or dependency identity changed after creation. Real experiments also fail closed when no Python lockfile hash exists. Prompt and profile files are snapshotted; mutating those files does not fail-close execution. Pricing-file mutation does not change an already-created experiment.
- `live_smoke_v1` remains the minimal paid provider smoke. `graph_live_smoke_v1` version 3 is the separate, $0.50-capped operational smoke for the complete graph path and remains byte-for-byte unchanged on `importance_weighted_log_odds_v1`. `graph_forecaster_v1` version 9 retains both deterministic private-V1 gates and relationship-aware aggregation, and adds only grounded Scenario Synthesis relative to version 8. The smoke profile's larger GPT-5 completion envelope is not a larger visible graph allowance or a higher run ceiling; no private-V1 gate, relationship-aware aggregation, or scenario call is applied to it. Neither smoke profile is selected by scientific evaluation defaults, and successful offline or live execution would demonstrate operational behavior only, not forecasting quality. No paid graph smoke was executed in this repair.
- Candidate backup handling rejects deterministic access-wall and challenge content before model extraction and retains only sanitized attempt metadata. The private-V1 evidence gate closes a minimum deterministic provenance, extraction, host-diversity, selected-node, critical-node, and graph-weight coverage boundary. It does not determine topical entailment, factual correctness, independence beyond normalized item/URL/host identity, or whether the frozen thresholds improve accuracy. The historical-evidence release prevents network/current-page fallback during a real backtest, but cannot prove substantive relevance, complete archive coverage, or source-license correctness. Broader semantic evidence sufficiency, probabilistic scenario modeling, calibration, a certified real corpus, a populated frozen historical-evidence corpus, and real-data evaluation remain open V1 gates.
- Claims created before migration `20260826_0024` retain their content and temporal provenance but have extraction method `unknown_legacy`; source class and host are backfilled only when deterministically recoverable. `unknown_legacy` evidence cannot satisfy structured or primary requirements, so a production run that reuses legacy claims may fail closed until new evidence is collected. The migration does not fabricate stronger history.
- Evidence Sufficiency Assessments are immutable and input-hashed. That protects audit integrity but means an already-assessed run cannot be silently re-evaluated after any cited evidence, graph weight, plan, or deterministic provenance change; a conflicting hash is a hard persistence error.
- Material-node completeness checks only a strict importance frontier. It prevents a lower-weight included or selected node from displacing a strictly higher-weight one, but it does not prove that graph weights or declared relationships are correct, require every equal-weight frontier node, enforce parent/dependency completeness, or model conditional interactions. Relationship gaps remain warnings in that gate. The later grounded scenario stage explains included relationships but does not numerically enforce them; the relationship-aware aggregator uses direct declared links only, with an equal split and no learned or causal parameter. The immutable assessment fails closed on conflicting inputs and makes no forecasting-quality claim.
- `relationship_mass_conserving_log_odds_v1` conserves declared graph importance and treats omitted or unrepresented mass as neutral log odds at probability `0.5`. It does not estimate conditional probabilities, relationship direction strength, transitive effects, common-cause dependence, scenario overlap, calibration, or the truth of graph-authored weights and links. A wrong graph can therefore produce a precisely auditable but methodologically wrong allocation. Forecasting quality remains unevaluated on a frozen real dataset.
- `private_v1_scenario_synthesis_v1` deterministically grounds model-authored prose to included nodes and their cited eligible claims, but grounding does not prove that the prose is complete, causally correct, or decision-relevant. The three pathways have no probabilities or weights and cannot improve or modify the numerical forecast. Strict output, packet-size, token-reserve, grounding, or coverage failures stop before aggregation after one logical call and no semantic retry. Immutable success/failure artifacts protect audit history but require a new Forecast Run for changed inputs. Stubbed success proves only request, validation, persistence, and invariance behavior; live reliability and forecasting quality remain unproven.
- Stubbed strict-output validation demonstrates request-shape, token-cap, failure-audit, and deterministic graph-processing behavior only. It does not establish live provider reliability or forecasting quality.
- Successful graph-generation audits contain deliberately limited diagnostics and cannot reconstruct the provider response or model reasoning. Graphs created before migration `20260825_0023` remain explicitly unaudited. Reusing one of those graphs does not issue a new graph request or fabricate historical diagnostics.
- The preserved FL-R005 operational smoke covered only 3 of 8 graph nodes with cited Evidence Claims. That 3/8 coverage remains visible in reports as a material research limitation; successful graph execution and improved audit identity do not turn it into a forecasting-quality pass.
- Historical evidence is accepted only after the final Wayback capture is verified. A rejected final capture keeps the actual archived original URL, final snapshot URL/timestamp, and failure reason. Incomplete archive coverage still means missing evidence, not proof of absence.
- The frozen evidence provider is deterministic and offline, but structural isolation is not encryption. A local database or bundle administrator can inspect audit and scoring stores. Non-redistributable source bytes must stay outside Git, and the manifest's licensing metadata is an audit assertion rather than a legal determination.
- Publication dates are optional because many otherwise usable live pages do not expose reliable publication metadata. For those pages, ForecastLab records that the content-hashed page was observed during the live run and labels the limitation visibly; retrieval time is never presented as publication time.
- Live retrieval-basis evidence proves current run-time availability only. It does not establish original publication time, prior page contents, or historical stability. Backtests cannot use retrieval time as eligibility proof and still require a verified final archive snapshot or explicitly supported immutable historical timestamp at or before the cutoff.
- Partial forecasts are scored separately from full forecasts. Mixing them into a single headline success rate is a defect.
- Built-in synthetic datasets are versioned. Older fixture versions remain as archived history when content changes.
- Search and fetch caches are per run. They do not survive a rerun after a watch change.
- FL-QF001 is cancelled. ForecastLab intentionally retains a question-first workflow; it has no Question Feed, practice league, market/community probability display, automatic scheduler, or automatic rerun service.
- The frozen frontend dependency lock retains three known high-severity advisories involving `next`, nested `postcss`, and `sharp`. The available remediation is a breaking Next upgrade and remains outside this task; critical advisories were zero in the preserved fresh-checkout verification.
- Native-provenance corpus V3 can collect timestamped native historical records
  into a private, outcome-blinded candidate corpus, but those records still
  require independent human review, outcome adjudication, licensing review, and
  leakage review before they can enter a frozen evaluation release. It does not
  change the preserved 3/5 live operational acceptance failure and it supplies
  no forecasting-quality or calibration result.
